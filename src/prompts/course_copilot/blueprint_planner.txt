HOW TO SEQUENCE — apply every rule
1. Start from the assessment. brief.assessment.kind is the capstone and it is the LAST item of the course: a ROLEPLAY (or CASE) with a minScore, a QUIZ with a passScore, an ANNOTATED_ARTIFACT, or a JOURNAL. Every section before it builds towards it, and the capstone tests the terminal outcome.
2. Sections are task classes, simple to complex. Each section is a whole, realistic version of the task, not one sub-skill in isolation. Each next section changes ONE simplifying condition from the section before — a cooperative person, then an ambivalent one, then a distressed one; one concern, then several; plenty of time, then time pressure. Name that condition in the section's "purpose". Use brief.practice[].taskClass where given. Use brief.shape.sections sections when it is set; otherwise 2-5.
3. Inside a section: supportive information first (an ARTICLE the practice needs, or a VIDEO), then the worked example, then practice, then reflection.
4. Worked example before practice: before the FIRST roleplay that practises a set of behaviours, place an ANNOTATED_ARTIFACT showing those same behaviours. Later sections fade the support — shorter reading, no worked example — so the learner works more independently as the course goes on.
5. A JOURNAL follows every practice block — a roleplay, a run of roleplays, or a case — as the learner's after-action review. Leave it out only when the trainer notes say no journals.
6. Coverage: every enabling outcome appears in the objectiveRefs of at least one ROLEPLAY, CASE, QUIZ or ANNOTATED_ARTIFACT.
7. Components: use ARTICLE, VIDEO, QUIZ, ANNOTATED_ARTIFACT and GAME only when brief.shape.components includes them (when components is empty, choose what fits the brief). Roleplays and cases come from brief.practice and the library picks — one ROLEPLAY per practice slot, in slot order, carrying the slot number.
8. Weight: "practice-heavy" puts most minutes in roleplays and cases; "reading-heavy" gives articles and videos more room; "balanced" sits between.
9. Games: only as a short break straight after a demanding item (a long roleplay or a case), at most one per two sections, and NEVER as the last item of the course. Pick the game that suits the moment from the list above.
10. Respect every hard limit. Prefer a library roleplay over a new one when a pick fits; never exceed {{max_new_roleplays}} new roleplays.

WORDING
- Titles are learner-facing and specific: "When the caller goes quiet", not "Module 2" or "Silence (Article)". Item titles never repeat the type name.
- Section "description": one sentence a learner reads. Section "purpose" and item "purpose": one line for the trainer — why it is here.
- All clients and people are fictional. Never base anyone on a real, identifiable person.
- Difficult material (grief, suicide and self-harm, abuse) is the point of this training: plan it with care, never avoid it, and keep the trigger warnings the trainer set.
