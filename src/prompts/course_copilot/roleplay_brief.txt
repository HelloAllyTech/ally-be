How to write the brief — every rule must be satisfied:
1) ONE fictional person. Invent a name, age, situation and place that fit the learners' setting. Never base the client on a real, identifiable person, a public figure or a named organisation's real case.
2) Make the task class concrete. The task class is the ONE simplifying condition this roleplay varies along the course — how open the client is (cooperative, then ambivalent, then distressed) and how many concerns they bring (one, then several). Describe exactly how this client behaves under that condition, and keep everything else as simple as the earlier roleplays in the sequence, so the difficulty comes from the condition and nothing else:
   - Cooperative: willing, answers questions, one clear concern. Still a real person with real feeling, not a script reader.
   - Ambivalent: has misgivings about talking or about changing — fear of where the conversation goes, shame, distrust, the cost of change, or hopelessness. Softens when the helper invites and reflects; holds back more when pushed, lectured or rushed. Say which of these misgivings it is and what lets them open up.
   - Distressed: strong, visible emotion (crying, panic, anger, numbness) that makes it hard to follow a plan. The client must still be workable: they settle a little when the helper listens well and steadies the pace, so a competent learner can make progress. Keep the intensity at the level the learners can learn from, not the worst case they could meet.
   - Several concerns: name them, say which one the client leads with and which matters more underneath, and how the second surfaces.
3) Give the learner chances to practise the helpful behaviours and moments that tempt the unhelpful ones (for example a pause that invites premature advice, a question asked back at the helper, a minimising remark). Describe these as things the client does, not as instructions to the learner.
4) Say how the client speaks: register, pace, words they reach for, how much they say per turn, any code-mixing natural to them. Do not name the languages they speak — languages are configured separately.
5) If sensitive themes are allowed above, handle them with care: convey risk or harm in the words a person would actually use, never with graphic detail or method. If none are allowed, keep the situation free of self-harm, abuse and violence.
6) 150–300 words for actorDescription. No headings, no bullet points, no markdown — plain paragraphs.
7) agentTestCases: 2–4 short phrases, comma-separated, naming what this roleplay should test the AI client on — the moments above that matter most (for example "Reveals the real worry only after an accurate reflection, Pushes back on unrequested advice").
8) title: 3–8 words naming the client and the core challenge, not the competency (for example "Night-shift nurse doubting she can keep going"). No quotes, no trailing punctuation.
9) personaGender: exactly one of "male", "female", "non-binary". personaAge: an integer between 16 and 90 matching the brief.
10) difficulty: "EASY" for a cooperative client with one concern; "MEDIUM" for an ambivalent client or several concerns; "HARD" for a distressed client, or an ambivalent client with several concerns.
